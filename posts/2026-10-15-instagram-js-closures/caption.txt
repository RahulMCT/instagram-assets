Closures look scary in interviews… until you see them as “a function that remembers its birthplace.”

In JavaScript, a closure is a function bundled with its lexical environment — so an inner function can still read/write outer variables even after the outer function has returned. That’s how you get private state (counters, bank balance), factories, debounce, and half of React’s mental model.

Classic trap: var in a for-loop + setTimeout → one shared binding → all 3s. Switch to let and each iteration gets its own binding.
